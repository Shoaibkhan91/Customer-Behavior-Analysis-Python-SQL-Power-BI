# 🛍️ Customer Shopping Behavior Analysis

An end-to-end **Data Analytics project** focused on understanding customer purchasing behavior, sales performance, subscription patterns, and business opportunities using **Python, SQL Server, and Power BI**.

The project follows a complete analytics workflow:

**Raw Data → Data Cleaning → Feature Engineering → SQL Analysis → Power BI Dashboard → Business Insights**

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Understanding customer behavior is essential for improving sales, customer retention, and subscription growth.

This project analyzes customer shopping data to answer important business questions such as:

* How much revenue is generated by different customer groups?
* Do subscribers spend more than non-subscribers?
* Which products and categories perform best?
* How frequently do customers purchase?
* Which customer segments generate the most revenue?
* Are repeat buyers more likely to subscribe?

The analysis was performed in three major stages:

### 1️⃣ Python — Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering

Used **Pandas and NumPy** to clean the raw dataset, handle missing values, standardize columns, and create useful analytical features.

### 2️⃣ SQL Server — Business Analysis

Loaded the cleaned data into **SQL Server** and answered 10 business questions using SQL techniques such as subqueries, CASE statements, CTEs, aggregations, and window functions.

### 3️⃣ Power BI — Dashboard & Visualization

Built an interactive dashboard to present KPIs, customer behavior, category performance, and subscription insights.

# 🧹 Step 1: Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering

The raw customer shopping dataset was cleaned and prepared using **Python and Pandas**.

### Data Cleaning

* Inspected the dataset using `info()`, `describe()` and `isna()`
* Identified missing values
* Filled **37 missing Review_Rating values** using the average rating of the respective product category
* Standardized column names to lowercase
* Renamed `Purchase_Amount_(USD)` to `purchase_amount`
* Removed duplicate information by dropping `promo_code_used`, as it was identical to `discount_applied`

### Feature Engineering

Created additional features to support business analysis:

* **Age_group**

  * Young Adult
  * Adult
  * Middle Age
  * Senior

* **purchase_frequency_days**

  * Converted purchase frequency into a numerical representation of days

The cleaned dataset was then loaded into SQL Server using **SQLAlchemy and PyODBC**.

# 🗄️ Step 2: SQL Server Analysis

After cleaning the data, I loaded it into **SQL Server** and answered 10 business questions using SQL.

| #  | Business Question                                             | SQL Concepts                      |
| -- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 1  | Total revenue by male vs. female customers                    | `GROUP BY`, `SUM`                 |
| 2  | Customers who used a discount but spent more than the average | Subquery                          |
| 3  | Top 5 products by average review rating                       | `TOP`, `AVG`, `ROUND`             |
| 4  | Average purchase amount: Standard vs. Express shipping        | `WHERE`, `AVG`                    |
| 5  | Do subscribers spend more than non-subscribers?               | `GROUP BY`, `COUNT`, `AVG`, `SUM` |
| 6  | Top 5 products by percentage of discounted purchases          | `CASE WHEN`, `SUM`, `COUNT`       |
| 7  | Customer segmentation: New, Returning, Loyal                  | `CASE WHEN`, Subquery             |
| 8  | Top 3 most purchased products in each category                | Window Function, `PARTITION BY`   |
| 9  | Are repeat buyers also likely to subscribe?                   | `WHERE`, `GROUP BY`               |
| 10 | Revenue contribution of each age group                        | Subquery, Percentage Calculation  |

# 💡 Key Business Insights

### 1. Subscription Opportunity

Only **27% of customers are subscribers**, while **73% are non-subscribers**.

This indicates an opportunity to investigate what benefits could encourage more customers to join the subscription program.

### 2. Category Performance

**Clothing** generates the highest revenue and sales among the analyzed categories, followed by Accessories, Footwear, and Outerwear.

### 3. Customer Demographics

The **Young Adult** customer group contributes the highest revenue, with **Middle Age** customers also contributing significantly.

### 4. Customer Loyalty

Approximately **80% of customers fall into the Loyal segment**, while only around **2% are New customers**.

This highlights the importance of balancing customer retention with new customer acquisition.

### 5. Discount Behavior

Discount usage is relatively evenly distributed across products. The top five products have discounted-purchase shares ranging from approximately **47% to 50%**.

# ✅ Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following opportunities could be explored:

### 1. Grow Subscription Memberships

Introduce member-exclusive benefits such as early access, free shipping, and special offers.

### 2. Increase Average Order Value

Create product bundles, particularly combining **Clothing and Accessories**, to encourage customers to purchase multiple products.

### 3. Personalize Discounts

Instead of applying the same discounts to everyone, use customer purchase behavior to create more targeted offers.

### 4. Improve Customer Engagement

Focus marketing and loyalty campaigns on high-value customer segments while creating strategies to re-engage less active customers.

### 5. Reward Loyal Customers

Introduce loyalty rewards and personalized offers to retain high-value customers.

### 6. Improve Review Data Quality

Encourage customers to submit ratings after purchases to improve both customer feedback and the quality of future analysis.
